# Monitoring and corrective action

## Purpose trigger and ownership

Use WF09 for an incident, complaint or failed assessment and WF10 for continuing review. Operations owns intake and immediate service response; the service owner funds correction; QA verifies the repair. Legal/compliance owners reconsider affected legal conclusions and claims. Inputs are the event, affected service/build, current records, severity evidence, operational permissions and support routes. The process must work between committee meetings and outside a planned audit cycle.

## Make monitoring decision-relevant

Review material releases, content changes, new tasks/territories, platform or assistive-technology changes, suppliers, legal/standards updates, research, support themes, contradictions and approaching expiry. Set a proportionate local cadence and event triggers; a calendar schedule alone will miss consequential changes. Recheck standards status before relying on a citation or issuing a new claim.

Capture complaints through accessible channels. Zero complaints cannot show accessibility: people may be unable to report, lack alternatives or have abandoned the service. Distinguish observation, reported experience and inferred cause. Protect personal details while retaining enough context to reproduce the barrier. Route serious consequences promptly through the incident authority. For an in-scope service, Article 13(4) requires corrective measures and immediate notification of non-compliance to the competent authorities where it is provided; use the verified jurisdiction procedure without delaying for an ordinary review cycle.

## Triage and protect people

Identify the essential task, who is affected, whether independent/private use is lost, severity, frequency, reach, consequence, workaround and compounded burden. Do not assign low priority simply because the problem concerns satisfaction or a small population. A modal intentionally containing focus is not automatically a keyboard trap; assess whether the person can operate and leave it appropriately.

Within existing delegation, apply reversible protections such as a verified accessible alternative or rollback. Confirm availability, privacy, cost, timeliness and equal service entitlement. Do not assume that a telephone workaround is equivalent. Record actions actually taken separately from recommendations, including owner, time and observed effect. Above-threshold, irreversible or legally consequential action follows the proper authority.

## Correct causes and verify outcomes

Create an issue linked to requirement, execution, evidence, outcome and affected decisions. Specify reproduction, target release, action, owner, due date, dependencies and expected evidence. Repair the shared cause where possible: component, content rule, integration, architecture, design pattern or assessment infrastructure. Similar-looking failures need a reviewed causal relationship before being merged.

Retest the affected conditions and relevant adjacent states using the declared methods and environment. A closed ticket or successful deployment is not proof of repair. Preserve the original failure and a new execution. If the process required real research, the absence of fresh participant evidence remains visible; synthetic scenarios cannot fill it. A failed repair reopens action rather than silently reusing an earlier approval.

Reassess exceptions and limitations on change or expiry. Renewal requires evidence and the correct decision-maker, not an automated extension. If adverse evidence undermines a conclusion, mark the affected claim for review and arrange amendment or withdrawal through WF08. Immediate protective communication requires the appropriate incident authority; this repository grants no external messaging permission.

## Measure process and AI accuracy

Report requirement/assessment coverage separately from legal conclusions, outcomes and process health. Define scope, period and denominator for escapes, recurrence, age, verified resolution and support dependence. A rise in reported barriers may reflect better detection. Use changes to improve prevention rather than discourage reporting.

For AI evaluation, use the metric definitions in the workflow file: unsupported positive conclusions, missed applicable requirements/failures, correct citations/mappings, correct escalation/abstention, assessment coverage and unauthorised actions. A reference decision needs competent human review. Report unknowns and abstention alongside accuracy; an agent cannot appear accurate merely by avoiding hard cases. Set adoption thresholds locally before evaluation, not after observing results.

Different maturity models serve different questions. Keep model ID and version: the governance RACI's four-level model is not the five-level AI maturity model. Do not convert levels automatically or treat maturity as legal certification.

## Pilot evidence and next workflow

The included service cycle is synthetic. To establish an operational process, run a bounded real pilot through intake, source changes, procurement/delivery, manual assessment, research as needed, correction, retest, claim control and monitoring. Include a failed dependency, interrupted execution, escalation and recovery. Observe whether records, permissions, privacy and decisions work; assign lessons and retest changes.

Only describe a process as proven within the context actually evidenced. Human review of this package, populated national adapters, actual tool/provider contracts, specialist methods and an independent evaluation set remain adoption prerequisites. Retain monitoring events, corrected executions, decision updates and audit evidence. Route changed scope to WF02, revised planning to WF03, and source changes to WF01; independent authorised work continues while the affected conclusion is held.
