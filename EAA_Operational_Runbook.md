# European Accessibility Act operational runbook

**A single-document guide to managing scope, policy, procurement, delivery, testing, customer outcomes, evidence, reporting and continuing improvement.**

Prepared for [Double-FW's EAA Compliance Approach repository](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach). This runbook brings together its six guides and preserves its ten workflow IDs, result vocabulary and twenty customer-outcome IDs. People can follow the process without running software. Authorised agents can assist with the same process within a genuine organisational mandate.

The intended result is an accessible product or service with traceable decisions and demonstrable customer outcomes. The method distinguishes legal requirements, technical standards, contractual/adopted policy requirements and additional experience ambitions. An organisation must appoint its actual owners, confirm national requirements and adopt its operating arrangements; this document does not supply product-specific approval.

## 1. Start here

1. **Choose the product, service or customer journey.** Identify its legal entity, operator role, consumers, countries, channels, suppliers and current release/configuration.
2. **Name the service owner.** Confirm who can fund work, who decides legal scope, who classifies assessment evidence and who can approve a claim.
3. **Verify the source position through WF01.** Record current national law, applicable dates and the standards editions/citations being relied on.
4. **Decide applicability through WF02.** Keep uncertain legal facts visible. Do not treat a commercial website or European audience alone as sufficient classification.
5. **Plan and deliver through WF03–WF06.** Connect requirements, procurement, design, technical assessment and customer research.
6. **Review, communicate and maintain through WF07–WF10.** Base each conclusion on the actual evidence for its stated scope and version.

**For an urgent barrier:** start WF09 immediately through the existing service/incident authority. Protect people, investigate notification duties and preserve evidence while independent scope and planning work continues. A regular governance meeting is not a reason to delay required action.

### Working principles

- Keep legal applicability, technical results, customer outcomes, release decisions and public claims as separate connected records.
- Assess complete tasks, including entry, authentication, payment, support, error recovery and exit, where relevant.
- Include disabled people in discovery and evaluation; participants contribute expertise without accepting organisational liability.
- Preserve missing, negative, contradictory and severe minority evidence. High satisfaction does not erase a failed requirement.
- Use automation for what it can establish and obtain the residual manual, assistive-technology, specialist and participant evidence.
- A template, workflow definition, passing schema or supplier statement is not evidence that a real activity succeeded.

## 2. Establish the right scope

The EAA is Directive (EU) 2019/882. Its national implementing measures determine many operational details. Its coverage is broader than websites and apps, but it does not apply independently to every digital system.

### Categories to check

| Route | Products, services or functions to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Article 2(1), products | Consumer general-purpose computer hardware and operating systems; payment terminals; specified self-service terminals such as ATMs, ticketing/check-in and interactive information terminals; consumer electronic-communication and audiovisual-access terminal equipment; e-readers. |
| Article 2(2), consumer services | Electronic communications, subject to the machine-to-machine exclusion; access to audiovisual media services; specified passenger-transport elements; consumer banking; e-books and dedicated software; e-commerce. |
| Article 2(3), emergency answering | Answering communications to 112 by the appropriate public safety answering point, with its applicable implementation arrangements. |
| Connected surfaces | Websites, web apps, native apps, desktop software, documents, media, physical controls, terminals, communication interfaces and support, to the extent connected to an applicable duty. |

For urban, suburban and regional transport, check the narrower route concerning relevant interactive self-service terminals. Distinguish audiovisual programmes from services providing access to them. Include third-party integrations; outsourcing does not automatically remove responsibility.

An internal employee system may fall outside this EAA consumer-service route while still engaging equality, accommodation, procurement, privacy or other duties. AI functionality can add separate AI Act responsibilities. Keep those legal profiles separate rather than applying an EAA exemption to another law.

### Dates, exclusions and limitations

Record the exact legal basis and affected scope for each decision:

| Issue | Required scope check |
| --- | --- |
| General application | Most national implementing measures apply from 28 June 2025; confirm the actual category and national rule. |
| Older content | Article 2(4) contains specific conditions for pre-28 June 2025 recorded media/office formats, maps, third-party content and archives. These are not blanket exclusions for old services or outsourced components. |
| Microenterprise services | The definition concerns fewer than ten persons **and** annual turnover **or** annual balance-sheet total not exceeding €2 million. Verify the full enterprise facts and service exemption; product duties differ. |
| Fundamental alteration | Assess the claimed significant change to the basic nature under Article 14; inconvenience or a tight schedule is not the test. |
| Disproportionate burden | Apply Article 14 and Annex VI, including resources, costs/benefits, accessibility funding and the actual assessment/documentation/notification conditions. |
| Service burden reviews | Renew on relevant alteration, authority request and at least every five years, as required. |
| Legacy service products | Article 32 provides a qualified transition ending 28 June 2030 for relevant products already lawfully used in providing similar services. |
| Existing contracts | Contracts agreed before 28 June 2025 may continue without alteration until expiry, but no longer than five years from that date, subject to the applicable route. |
| Existing terminals | A Member State may permit specified pre-existing terminals through economic life, with the Directive's twenty-year outer limit from entry into use. Verify national adoption. |

Do not turn the 2030 provisions into a universal grace period. For limitations, record continuing duties, affected requirements, evidence, notification, review and expiry separately. A valid limitation is not the same as accepting a defect.

### Route Annex I correctly

Sections I–II address products; III general service requirements; IV additional requirements for specific services; V emergency answering; VI the Article 24(2) route; and VII functional performance criteria. Select the exact clauses for the scoped functions.

Section VII addresses relevant functions not covered in Sections I–VI. Its alternative-technical-solution route has conditions, including meeting the accessibility requirement and achieving equivalent or increased accessibility for foreseeable use. It is not a general substitute based only on customer preference or a favourable survey.

Primary source: [Directive 2019/882, Articles 2–4, 14, 31–32 and Annex I](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng).

## 3. Assign ownership and adopt the policy controls

The following are functions to assign locally, not people already appointed or a requirement to create multiple departments or agents. Combine roles where proportionate while preserving competence and meaningful challenge.

| Activity | Accountable function | Work and contributions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programme remit, budget and adoption | Executive sponsor/service owner | Fund delivery, assessment, support and specialist gaps. |
| Legal scope and statutory limitations | Authorised legal owner | Determine national applicability and approve exact scope/limitation decisions. |
| Requirements, tests and coverage | QA lead | Maintain mappings, valid methods, actual results and coverage limits. |
| Interaction, content and branding | Design/content owner | Specify accessible foundations, patterns, states, communication and recovery. |
| Implementation and repair | Engineering/delivery owner | Implement requirements, address causes and provide versioned evidence. |
| Procurement and supplier acceptance | Service owner within delegation | Set contractual evidence, integration acceptance, correction and change arrangements. |
| Research | Research lead | Own method, recruitment, interpretation and participant inclusion. |
| Privacy and evidence handling | Relevant privacy/data owner | Establish lawful processing, permissions, access, retention and disclosure controls. |
| Conformity conclusions | Authorised legal/compliance owner | Decide the bounded legal conclusion using current scope and evidence. |
| Public claims | Authorised claim owner | Approve exact text, scope, evidence, artifact and channel. |
| Live monitoring and correction | Operations/service owner | Maintain accessible intake, triage, action, retest and review. |

### Minimum policy decisions

Adopt and fund controls for:

1. Legal applicability and a named owner for each product/service.
2. Applicable accessibility baselines and clearly labelled stronger experience targets.
3. Accessible procurement, supplier evidence, correction and change notification.
4. Design/brand systems, complete-journey delivery and layered assessment.
5. Inclusive research, privacy and protected evidence handling.
6. Release decisions, legal limitations and internal departures with distinct authority.
7. Accessible support/complaints and required statutory notifications.
8. Continuing monitoring, correction, evidence retention, claims and withdrawal.
9. AI/agent delegation, source verification, review and recovery where used.

The adopted policy should identify scope, owner/deputy, approving authority, resources, effective date, evidence and review triggers. Do not claim that adopting this runbook itself establishes conformity.

## WF01: Source verification and remediation

**Trigger:** a source change, failed link, review due or a source being relied on for a new decision.  
**Accountable:** source owner.  
**Inputs:** exact source/version, relevant claim or mapping and any remediation finding.

1. Retrieve the exact official or owning source. Record authority, title, URL, version, date, language, reviewed scope and rights.
2. Check the relevant national law, standard edition and legislation-specific citation. Preserve failed access and uncertainty.
3. Compare the proposed use with the source's actual scope and conditions. Keep law, standard, contract, policy and recommended practice distinct.
4. Correct the affected interpretation or mapping and identify dependent requirements, evidence and claims.
5. Validate the changed records/links and record the disposition: current, superseded, historical, unavailable or requiring further review, with reasons.

**Standards decision:** publication, contractual adoption and Official Journal citation are different facts. The repository's standards register, checked on 1 November 2026, records EN 301 549 v4.1.1 as published in September 2026 but does not establish its EAA citation. It records v3.2.1's WAD reference separately. Do not transfer that WAD status to EAA or describe the old review as a fresh legal-citation audit.

For v4.1.1 assessment routing, consult its actual Annex A.2 Tables A.1–A.5, EAA relationship Annex ZB, WAD relationship Annex ZA and assessment Annex C. Verify the actual edition before using these locators. WCAG 2.2 A/AA is the repository's proposed web delivery profile, not a version directly named by the Directive.

**Output and exit:** a dated source disposition with owned gaps and updated dependent records. An unavailable source holds only the conclusion depending on it; independent authorised work continues.

**Reference:** [G02](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/02-requirements-and-assessment.md) and [standards status](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/control/standards-status.yaml).

## WF02: Intake and applicability

**Trigger:** a new/acquired/changed service or product, territory, operator role or legal assumption.  
**Accountable:** authorised legal owner; service owner supplies facts and funds the response.  
**Inputs:** service/product facts, jurisdiction sources and current legal context.

1. Describe the legal entity, operator roles, consumers, intended functions, channels, support, suppliers, release and relevant dates.
2. Make one jurisdiction record per relevant Member State: national law/version, official source, authority, duties, dates, language, reporting and retention requirements.
3. Apply Article 2's exact definitions/category. Inventory connected digital and physical functions without assuming everything is independently covered.
4. Route the relevant Annex I clauses. Assess exclusions, transitions and statutory limitations separately with their conditions and evidence.
5. Record included/excluded boundaries, rationale, uncertainties and other legal regimes. Never use an EAA out-of-scope finding to remove equality or other duties.
6. Obtain the exact-version scope decision from the authorised legal owner, or record `legal review required` for unresolved facts or interpretation.

Use the scope findings `in scope`, `partly in scope`, `out of scope` or `legal review required`. Missing facts are not evidence of an exemption.

**Output and exit:** applicability and any legal-limitation records linked to the relevant jurisdiction. WF03 proceeds on the approved scope; unresolved legal issues remain visible while independent design or evidence collection continues within authority.

**Reference:** [G01](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/01-scope-and-applicability.md).

## WF03: Requirements and evidence planning

**Trigger:** approved scope, a new baseline or a material requirements change.  
**Accountable:** QA lead for coverage; service owner funds delivery; legal owner controls legal interpretation.  
**Input:** approved applicability, capability inventory, current national/technical sources and intended release.

1. Create one requirement per relevant source clause and scoped subject. Retain original meaning separately from interpretation.
2. Define observable acceptance conditions and who will implement, assess and decide them.
3. Map legal requirements to the applicable technical clauses, tests and additional customer outcomes. State partial coverage and residual evidence.
4. Choose methods for web/native software, documents, communications, media, hardware and closed functionality where relevant. Assign competent specialists for gaps.
5. Define states, tasks, sample rationale, environments, dependencies, evidence, privacy arrangements, timing and resources before execution.
6. Review coverage and fund unresolved manual/specialist work before the affected acceptance decision.

Maintain many-to-many relationships: one legal requirement can require several tests; one test can support several scoped requirements. Similar titles or numbering do not establish equivalence.

| Example legal requirement | Useful evidence | Evidence still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annex I III(c), in-scope service interfaces | Applicable keyboard criteria and actual operation. | Other applicable criteria, states, channels, complete tasks and wider legal duties. |
| Annex I IV(e)(ii), banking information | Qualified content/comprehension review against the B2 condition. | WCAG AA alone does not establish this complexity requirement; WCAG 3.1.5 is AAA and not a B2 equivalence rule. |
| Annex I IV(g)(iii), e-commerce identification/signature/payment | Relevant authentication and transaction tests, including applicable WCAG 3.3.8. | Actual security/payment/signature routes, accuracy, error recovery and other applicable duties. |

**Output and exit:** requirements, mappings and a usable assessment/evidence plan, with owners and gaps. A mapping is planning evidence, not an executed pass.

**Reference:** [G02](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/02-requirements-and-assessment.md).

## WF04: Procurement and supplier acceptance

**Trigger:** selection, renewal, acquisition or material supplier change.  
**Accountable:** service owner within actual procurement/release delegation.  
**Inputs:** scoped requirements, evidence expectations, supplier/product facts and permitted evaluation.

1. State the intended tasks, contexts, supported channels, requirements and acceptance evidence before commitment.
2. Distinguish an existing product from a promised future build. Process capability is useful but not evidence that an unfinished product conforms.
3. Review assessment version/date, exact scope, evaluator competence, test methods, sampling, failures, exclusions and changes since evaluation.
4. Exercise critical tasks in the intended integration, including authentication, payment, support and recovery where relevant. A supplier demo or declaration alone is insufficient.
5. Agree staged evidence, corrective action, responsibility, accessibility information, change notification, retesting, support, exit/migration and monitoring arrangements.
6. Record the service owner's acceptance decision, evidence, limitations, open issues and review triggers. Separate commercial acceptance from a legal conformity conclusion.

**Output and exit:** supplier evidence/acceptance record and owned unresolved issues. Continue WF05 for integrated delivery and WF07 for the evidence/conformity decision.

**Reference:** [G03](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/03-delivery-and-procurement.md).

## WF05: Design implementation and assessment

**Trigger:** build, component, content, configuration or integration change.  
**Accountable:** QA lead for assessment, with design/engineering/service owners retaining their delivery responsibilities.  
**Inputs:** requirements, mappings, valid methods, declared environment and action authority.

1. Specify the full journey and normal/error/recovery states before implementation: information, semantics, reading/focus order, labels, alternatives, timing, authentication, payment, privacy and support.
2. Use reviewed design-system decisions and reference implementations, verifying configured content and composition in the actual service.
3. Apply the same acceptance conditions to human- and AI-generated work. Investigate causes rather than patch only the visible symptom.
4. Run meaningful automation and required manual, keyboard, assistive-technology, visual/content and specialist evaluation.
5. Retain actual build, method, environment, expected/observed outcomes, raw results, scope limits, evidence and evaluator competence.
6. Create owned issues and correction/retest actions. Prepare whole-journey release evidence, including untested or blocked scope.

### Design and branding assets to organise

For every relevant foundation or practice—such as typography, colour, labels/names, alternative text and content order—identify guidance, requirements, manual methods, meaningful automation, residual human work, owner/version and dependencies.

For every pattern/component, identify its design requirements, technical techniques, delivery/evaluation methodology, versioned reference implementation, tests and user-oriented task scripts. Local ACT-style scripts complement formal conformance rules; they are not automatically approved W3C ACT rules.

Use accessible branding to preserve identification, trust, expectations, value, reinforced values and audience resonance. These are experience ambitions to evaluate; they do not add statutory WCAG criteria or turn satisfaction into a fifth formal POUR principle.

### Select the right assessment method

| Surface or question | Methods and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Website/web app/WebView | DOM/browser checks plus manual keyboard, focus, AT, content, visual and complete-process evaluation. A scan only establishes its inspected states/rules. |
| Native mobile/desktop app | Platform accessibility APIs/tools and actual supported devices, AT, input and settings. A web scan/emulation does not establish native access. |
| Documents and generated content | Structure, meaningful alternatives, language, reading order, export and real AT/content review. File existence does not establish usability. |
| Communications/media | Relevant real-time text, audio/video, access-feature accuracy, timing, transmission and player behaviour, with specialists. |
| Hardware/closed functionality | Applicable physical, sensory, reach/strength, timing, privacy and independent-use procedures using competent real-device assessment. |
| Customer outcomes | Task observation and appropriately designed research; technical assertions alone cannot establish comprehension or equivalent experience. |

Verify the actual automation engine/package, lockfile, effective rule inventory, configuration and known-failure checks. The source package contains different proposed axe versions and adopts no installed engine. Do not select a version or claim a coverage percentage from those historical proposals alone.

**Output and exit:** versioned executions, evidence, issues and coverage. Preserve failures and route to WF09; route current evidence to WF07. Use WF06 for the remaining experience questions.

**References:** [G02](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/02-requirements-and-assessment.md), [G03](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/03-delivery-and-procurement.md), [public QA entry point](https://github.com/Double-FW/QA_Accessibility_Testing) and [design/brand systems process](https://github.com/Double-FW/Accessibility_Programme_Runbooks/blob/main/framework/design-and-brand-systems.md).

## WF06: Inclusive research and outcome analysis

**Trigger:** a material experience question, proposed experience decision, change, complaint or new barrier.  
**Accountable:** research lead for method; privacy owner for data arrangements; service owner for funded action.  
**Inputs:** research question, selected outcomes, tasks/contexts, protocol, data permissions and actual instrument/export arrangements.

1. Select relevant IDSO outcomes from the table below and explain the choice. These are Double-FW organisational outcomes, not statutory EAA clause IDs.
2. Recruit relevant people, including disabled people unable to enter, finish or report through the existing service. Do not rely only on successful end-of-journey respondents.
3. Provide accessible participation, materials, consent, access support, breaks and communication choices. Involve people early enough to influence questions and decisions.
4. Collect only authorised data; define the legal basis, participation/recording permissions, provider roles, recipients, retention/deletion, reuse and small-cell protections before collection.
5. Observe complete realistic tasks, correct end states, assistance, interruption, recovery and abandonment. Combine observations with reports of understanding, effort, confidence, dignity and satisfaction.
6. Analyse distributions, missing responses, negative cases, uncertainty and coverage. Keep synthetic personas/examples separate from real participant evidence.
7. Record findings per selected IDSO outcome, linked to context/version and evidence, then route material barriers to WF09 and findings to WF07.

### Preserve the repository's customer-outcome IDs

| ID | Outcome focus | Example question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IDSO-01 | Perception | Can people perceive the relevant information and feedback? |
| IDSO-02 | Meaning | Are labels, symbols and messages meaningful? |
| IDSO-03 | Operation | Can people operate the necessary controls? |
| IDSO-04 | Navigation | Can people find, orientate and move through the service? |
| IDSO-05 | Understanding | Can people understand the information and consequences? |
| IDSO-06 | Technology/settings | Does the service work with relevant technology and chosen settings? |
| IDSO-07 | Safety | Can people use the service without unacceptable access-related harm? |
| IDSO-08 | Support | Can people obtain suitable, accessible help? |
| IDSO-09 | Accurate completion | Does the task end correctly, with understandable confirmation? |
| IDSO-10 | Effort/time | Is the demand reasonable in the task/context, including queues and recovery? |
| IDSO-11 | Recovery | Can people correct mistakes and resume after interruption? |
| IDSO-12 | Autonomy | Is support chosen rather than forced dependence? |
| IDSO-13 | Privacy/security/consent | Are privacy, security and informed choices maintained in accessible modes? |
| IDSO-14 | Equivalent service | Are material availability, quality, timing, cost, privacy and recourse comparable? |
| IDSO-15 | Continuity | Can people maintain context across sessions, steps and channels? |
| IDSO-16 | Confidence/control | Can people check progress and remain in control? |
| IDSO-17 | Dignity | Is the experience respectful and free from avoidable humiliation? |
| IDSO-18 | Satisfaction | Are people satisfied with the experience, for stated reasons and context? |
| IDSO-19 | Collaboration | Can people participate in relevant shared or assisted tasks? |
| IDSO-20 | Enjoyment, where material | Can people access the intended enjoyment or cultural benefit? |

Annex I VII's a–k functional lenses concern use without/with limited vision, colour perception, without/with limited hearing, without vocal capability, limited manipulation/strength, limited reach, photosensitive-seizure risk, limited cognition and privacy. Use their legal conditions for compliance; broader use as research coverage prompts is recommended practice. Privacy is assessed across accessible modes, not treated as a diagnosis or respondent category.

Ask voluntary task-relevant needs questions; allow overlapping selections, no need reported, prefer not to answer and missing responses. Consider fatigue, pain, fluctuation, language and environment without inventing additional statutory categories. Do not infer disability from keyboard, caption or screen-reader use. Never expose participants to unsafe flashing to obtain evidence.

### Define measures before collecting data

| Measure | Definition and reporting controls |
| --- | --- |
| Accurate completion | Verified correct end states divided by defined attempts; separate independent, chosen-assisted and forced-assisted completion. |
| Effort/time | Distribution for comparable tasks/skills/environments, including support queues and recovery; include observed/reported demand. |
| Satisfaction | Exact question/scale/endpoints, valid response count, distribution, context and missingness; not a proxy for correct independent completion. |
| Equivalence | Compare the material service benefits and burdens, rather than demand an identical interface. |

Overlapping groups cannot be summed into unique people. Means/counts do not supply missing variance. Before/after differences alone do not establish causation. Historical timing bands are not acceptance thresholds. Severe qualitative evidence can matter without estimating prevalence. Set local criteria before seeing results.

Validate the actual survey, accessibility, export schema, missing-data codes, period and provider operations. No functioning survey API or universal validated questionnaire is supplied by this runbook.

**Output and exit:** real outcome findings using the exact vocabulary in Section 5, with method, context, limits, negative cases and owned actions. Participation and collection consent do not by themselves establish all processing permissions.

**Reference:** [G04](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/04-customer-outcomes-and-research.md).

## WF07: Evidence and conformity recommendation

**Trigger:** a requirement/conformity decision, release review or proposed claim.  
**Accountable:** authorised legal/compliance owner; QA prepares the evidence assessment.  
**Inputs:** approved scope, current national/technical sources, requirements/mappings, executions, customer findings, limitations and issue history.

1. Reconcile the exact product/service, version, territory, channel, task and period across every evidence item.
2. Check source currency, interpretation, method validity, coverage, competence and evidence authenticity. Identify what each item does and does not establish.
3. Consider both supporting and contradictory evidence, including severe minority findings. Investigate differences in environment, population, task or release rather than discard disagreement.
4. Record a bounded conclusion for each requirement using the exact vocabulary in Section 5. Scope non-applicability needs the approved scope decision.
5. Prepare the aggregate decision with confidence and rationale, incomplete/failed coverage and unresolved legal questions. Narrow the scope or return `cannot conclude` where appropriate.
6. Obtain the authorised human's decision for the exact version and scope. Record authority, evidence, validity and review triggers.

| Route | Operational meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Repair and retest | Produce new implementation/execution evidence while retaining historical failure. |
| Temporary defect acceptance | Keep the failure, owner, compensating action, monitoring, review and expiry; this does not create legal conformity. |
| Policy departure | A different route from an adopted internal baseline needs justified outcome evidence and the proper authority. |
| Alternative technical solution | Use only a permitted legal route with its exact conditions and equivalent/increased accessibility evidence where required. |
| Statutory limitation | Use the actual legal assessment, affected scope, continuing duties and required notification/review. |

For products, check relevant operator technical documentation, assessment, EU declaration and marking duties under Articles 7–11, 16–18 and Annex IV. For services, use Article 13 and Annex V's information about fulfilment. Do not impose product CE documentation on a service by analogy.

**Output and exit:** requirement and aggregate conclusions linked to evidence and actual approval. Hold only the affected positive conclusion where material scope fails, is unassessed, has invalid methods/stale evidence or lacks authority. Independent authorised work can continue.

**Reference:** [G05](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/05-evidence-conformity-and-claims.md).

## WF08: Controlled claim and publication handoff

**Trigger:** creating, changing, publishing, correcting or withdrawing a public conformity/accessibility claim.  
**Accountable:** authorised claim owner.  
**Inputs:** supported approved conclusion, exact wording, actual channel/audience and publication authority.

1. Draft a precise statement identifying product/service, release, territory, languages/channels, assessed requirements, date, evidence and material limitations.
2. Link the statement to the relevant conformity decision. A research outcome, clean scan or supplier certificate is not a substitute.
3. Fix the exact UTF-8 text/artifact and calculate its SHA-256 where using the repository's claim record model.
4. Have the authorised human approve the exact version, scope, evidence, hash and channel. A schema-valid approval field cannot authenticate a person.
5. Check the artifact is unchanged and the authority remains valid. Hand it to the publication owner or publish only within actual standing/task-specific authority.
6. Use the schema's publication states `draft`, `handoff_ready`, `published` and `withdrawn`. Retain approval, actual handoff and correction as linked records/events rather than invent extra schema values. Keep the real location/receipt and published version where applicable.

A revised sentence, new evidence or changed service can require reconsideration. Do not claim publication occurred because a tool accepted a request or a workflow description says to publish.

**Output and exit:** exact claim, its approval and verified handoff/publication state. Monitoring through WF10 reopens the claim if the evidence changes.

**Reference:** [G05](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/05-evidence-conformity-and-claims.md) and [record contract](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/records/README.md).

## WF09: Incident correction and retest

**Trigger:** complaint, failed assessment, incident, severe outcome or evidence undermining a decision.  
**Accountable:** service owner; operations handles intake/protection, delivery repairs and QA verifies.  
**Inputs:** actual event, affected service/build/task, impact evidence and action authority.

1. Capture the report accessibly, including chosen communication, actual observation, affected task/context and protected evidence. Separate report, observation and inferred cause.
2. Triage impact: independent/private use, consequence, severity, reach, recurrence, cumulative burden and workaround. A small population does not automatically mean low priority.
3. Apply permitted reversible protection, such as a verified accessible route or safe rollback. Check availability, privacy, cost, timing and equal entitlement; a telephone alternative is not automatically equivalent.
4. Investigate applicable legal notification duties promptly. For an in-scope non-compliant service, Article 13(4) requires corrective measures and immediate information to competent national authorities where it is provided, including non-compliance and corrective action. Use the verified jurisdiction procedure; do not wait for a routine reporting cycle.
5. Create an issue linking requirements, executions, findings, impact, owner, due date, correction, temporary action, monitoring, review and expiry.
6. Fix the cause in the relevant component, content rule, design, integration, architecture or assessment process. Merge issues only when the causal relationship is supported.
7. Retest affected and adjacent states with appropriate current methods. Retain the original failure and a new execution. Ticket closure or deployment success is not repair evidence.
8. Reopen decisions and claims through WF07/WF08 where necessary. Record restart/closure only when the required evidence and authority exist.

Product nonconformity has its own operator and market-surveillance routes. Apply the actual role and national procedure rather than reuse the service process without analysis. Statutory limitations and internal exceptions require separate decisions.

**Output and exit:** documented protections, notifications where required, corrective action, retest and updated decisions/claims. An unsuccessful repair stays open.

**References:** [G06](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/06-monitoring-and-corrective-action.md) and [Article 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng).

## WF10: Change monitoring and reassessment

**Trigger:** release/content/supplier/platform/AT change, new task/territory, legal/standards update, research, complaint trend, contradiction, review or expiry.  
**Accountable:** operations owner, with the service owner funding action.  
**Inputs:** service inventory, current decisions/claims/issues and actual operational owners.

1. Monitor material changes and upcoming review/expiry. Use event triggers as well as a proportionate local cadence.
2. Identify the affected requirements, test/outcome evidence, limitations, conclusions, claims and service users.
3. Preserve adverse/minority evidence and the earlier state. Zero complaints does not prove access; reporting itself may be inaccessible.
4. Create a monitoring event with observation, impact, owner, next action/workflow, evidence and next review.
5. Route source change to WF01; scope change to WF02; revised planning to WF03; implementation to WF05; customer questions to WF06; failed use to WF09; conclusions/claims to WF07/WF08.
6. Verify correction, reassessment and any claim amendment/withdrawal. Renewal needs current evidence and authority rather than an automatic extension.

Suggested monthly operational and quarterly management reviews are local planning options, not statutory EAA reporting intervals. Preserve any exact legal review requirement, including Article 14 burden-assessment renewal conditions, separately.

For retirement or replacement, identify continuing duties, accessible migration/support, communications, supplier arrangements, evidence retention and authorised data deletion. Include remaining customers and dependencies until closure is verified.

**Output and exit:** owned monitoring events and completed linked actions, with historic evidence retained. A bounded real pilot should exercise ordinary delivery, failed dependencies, interruption, escalation, correction and recovery before describing the process as operationally proven.

**Reference:** [G06](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/06-monitoring-and-corrective-action.md).

## 5. Keep results and decisions distinct

### Technical test results

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `Pass` | Valid execution supports all applicable assertions in the declared test scope. |
| `Fail` | At least one applicable assertion demonstrably fails. |
| `Cannot tell` | Evidence is insufficient/ambiguous for the test conclusion. |
| `Not applicable` | The requirement/test precondition is not met, with a recorded reason. |
| `Not tested` | No valid execution evidence exists for the scope. |
| `Blocked` | A dependency or access condition prevents execution. |
| `Tool error` | The assessment tool/method malfunctioned; it is not a product pass. |

Preserve raw label, vocabulary/version and any explicit adapter. Under the repository adapter, `Not run` becomes `Not tested`, and `Needs review` becomes `Cannot tell`. Classify a blocked event as tool error only when the cause is an actual tool failure. Do not guess a missing cause.

A false requirement precondition differs from an invalid assessment assumption. An invalid method requires another appropriate method or an unresolved finding; it does not remove the requirement.

### Customer-outcome findings

Use `supported`, `partly supported`, `not supported`, `insufficient evidence`, `not evaluated` or `not relevant with rationale`. Preserve IDSO ID, observed/reported distinction, task/context, population, measures, negative cases, uncertainty and rationale.

`supported` is an outcome finding, not the legal requirement conclusion `supported as met`. Outcome non-relevance is not legal non-applicability.

### Legal requirement and aggregate decisions

For a requirement use `supported as met`, `not met`, `cannot conclude`, `not applicable by approved scope decision` or `not assessed`. Unresolved legal interpretation routes to `legal review required` in the relevant record; it is not a passing conclusion.

The aggregate conformity record uses `supported within stated scope`, `not supported` or `cannot conclude`. Confidence is `high`, `moderate`, `low` or `insufficient` with a rationale; it is not a calibrated probability of compliance.

Every record needs stable identity, kind, schema/record version, status, subject/release/territory/channel scope, sources, owner, dates, limitations, typed relationships and evidence references. Preserve synthetic/real classification, access and retention. Keep personal evidence and credentials out of public repository files.

| Source | Authority, version, location, status, verified date, reviewed scope and rights. |

Use relationships that explain meaning, such as supports, partially supports, contradicts, assesses, supersedes and triggers review. Maintain a reconstructable chain from scope to requirement to assessment to conclusion to claim and monitoring. New evidence supersedes or supplements earlier records; it does not erase adverse history.

### A practical assessment execution field set

For each assessed case capture:

1. Requirement/test/procedure IDs and versions.
2. Exact product/service build, configuration, channel and environment.
3. Objective, applicability, valid setup and expected observable result.
4. Actual inputs/actions and observed result, including errors/recovery.
5. Method, evaluator competence, date and protected evidence.
6. Raw/normalised result, interpretation and coverage limits.
7. Issue, owner, correction and linked retest.

## 7. Report for the correct purpose and audience

### Management report

Provide the reporting period, service/release/country/channel scope, accountable owner and decisions required. Show these separately:

- Applicable requirement inventory, assessed/untested/blocked coverage and evidence quality.
- Actual failures, unresolved legal scope/limitations and material service consequences.
- IDSO findings, accurate completion, effort, support dependence, dignity and satisfaction, with sample/denominator/uncertainty.
- Supplier gaps, resources, aged/recurrent issues and verified correction/retest.
- Public-information/claim currency, notifications, upcoming review/expiry and owned actions.

Do not present a count of automated rules as percentage legal compliance. Report severe minority failures and people missing from the evidence, alongside averages.

### Product documentation, service information and statutory reporting

| Purpose | Required route to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Product conformity | Relevant operator duties, technical documentation, assessment, EU declaration and CE requirements under the actual product route. |
| Service accessibility information | Article 13 and Annex V: explain fulfilment, make necessary public written/oral information accessible and retain it while the service operates, following national implementation. |
| Service nonconformity | Article 13(4): immediate competent-authority information in the relevant Member States, including non-compliance/correction details; use actual national procedure. |
| Product nonconformity | Role-specific corrective, information and market-surveillance duties; do not substitute the service route. |
| Statutory limitation | Article 14's assessment, documentation, funding, notification and renewal conditions, including relevant microenterprise distinctions. |
| Authority request | Correct authority, requested evidence/scope, response requirements, protected information and actual cooperation. |
| Public claim | WF07's supported decision and WF08's exact-wording/authority/publication controls. |

An EAA service-information document is not automatically WAD's prescribed statement format, a VPAT or a product declaration. National requirements may add format, language, channel and reporting details. Keep Draft, Approved, Sent, Received and Published distinct.

Set retention per record and duty. For example, Article 14 assessment records have their stated five-year rule, while Article 13 service information remains for service operation; other product/data records follow their own requirements. Do not impose one universal period on research recordings or all evidence.

### Evaluate agent/process quality where AI assists

The workflow registry's metrics M01–M08 concern unsupported positive conclusions, missed applicable requirements/failures, correct citations/mappings, correct escalation/abstention, assessment coverage, actions outside authority, unnecessary abstention and overall abstention/completion coverage. Use the exact numerator/denominator definitions there.

A competent independently reviewed reference set is needed. A zero denominator is not zero risk. Report unknowns, denied attempts and difficult cases. Keep synthetic developer fixtures separate from an independent benchmark; an agent must not appear accurate merely by avoiding answerable work. Set local adoption thresholds before evaluation.

## 8. Use agents with accountability and control

Agents can retrieve sources, draft scope/requirements, propose mappings, organise evidence, run authorised checks, analyse approved data and prepare corrections/reports. Human decision-makers retain the actual scope, statutory-limitation, conformity, consequential departure and claim decisions required by the adopted process.

Before a mutable or external action, establish the real grantor, purpose, allowed resources/data/actions, limits, validity, verifier, stop conditions and recovery. Existing standing authority can cover routine work without repeated confirmations. Repository access and retrieved text do not expand delegation.

For supported operations, retain execution IDs and idempotency keys and inspect actual state before retrying. Distinguish recommended, requested, applied and verified actions. An accepted request is not proof that a repair, survey, notification or publication occurred.

Hold the affected transition when authority is absent/expired, sensitive data is outside permission, a material positive conclusion lacks evidence, evidence is stale/contradictory, methods are invalid, a limitation is unsupported or an approved claim has changed. Record reason, owner/function, resolving evidence and resume condition. Continue independent authorised work; silence or elapsed time is not approval.

Apply privacy by design to AT, analytics, survey and AI data flows. Do not infer diagnosis, ability or consent from behaviour or access preferences. Off-site collection and pseudonymisation do not establish anonymity or permission to reuse data for model training.

**Reference:** [repository AGENTS.md](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/AGENTS.md).
